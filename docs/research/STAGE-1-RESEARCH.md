# ALMAS — Stage 1 Design Research

**Prepared:** 2026-11-16
**Status:** Research complete — awaiting client approval before Stage 2
**Scope:** 17 restaurant websites, 8 design-inspiration platforms, mobile checks, public-listing audit
**Screenshots:** `docs/research/screenshots/`

---

## 1. Purpose of this stage

Stage 1 establishes the visual and strategic territory for ALMAS before any interface is designed. It answers four questions:

1. What does the best contemporary hospitality design look like now?
2. Which of those patterns genuinely fit ALMAS, and which are fashionable but wrong?
3. What is ALMAS's real market position, based on verifiable information?
4. Which single creative direction should Stage 2 prototype?

This document is evidence, not a mood board. Every reference below was opened in a real browser and captured. Where a site could not be reached, that is stated rather than hidden.

---

## 2. Executive summary

**The strongest direction is a restrained, editorial system built around the idea of facets** — the many cuts of a diamond, and the many sides of ALMAS: grill, bread, hospitality, place. It combines:

- **Layout 01 — full-bleed photography** for atmosphere and desire.
- **Layout 03 — editorial menu** for clarity and craft.
- **A light "Porcelain" mode** for menu, visit and practical content.
- **A dark "Obsidian" mode** for immersive hero and evening atmosphere.
- **Instrument Serif / DM Sans / IBM Plex Mono** as the fixed typographic foundation.
- **Mineral plum, pomegranate, oxidized silver and smoked limestone** as measured accents — no gold, no gradients.

The research also surfaced a strategic tension that must be handled honestly:

> The brief asks for "Contemporary Gastronomic Luxury", but the verified menu is a casual grill/takeaway menu with a CHF 10–20 price band, and the public listing mentions drive-through and no-contact delivery. ALMAS can be presented with premium craft and confidence, but it must not pretend to be a fine-dining restaurant with invented tasting menus, awards or history.

The recommended answer is **"quiet premium, not fake luxury"**: exceptional typography, art-directed photography, generous spacing and precise information design applied to the real offer. This is exactly what Pizzeria Beddia, Pepe in Grani and St. JOHN do — they elevate everyday food through design and voice rather than through gold leaf and invented claims.

A second critical finding remains open: **the supplied menu contains no Afghan dishes.** The site cannot position ALMAS as an Afghan restaurant until the client confirms whether Afghan dishes exist on another menu. This affects the homepage headline, the About page and the entire content architecture.

## 3. Method and limitations

**Method**

- Desktop captures at 1440 × 900, mobile captures at 390 × 844.
- Each site was opened directly, not judged from a gallery thumbnail.
- Page text, navigation labels, menu structure and visible art direction were recorded.
- Screenshots were reviewed together as a contact sheet to compare composition, palette and typography fairly.

**Limitations**

- Some sites show cookie banners in captures. This is noted where it affects the reading.
- Siteinspire returned **403 Forbidden** and could not be reviewed.
- Just Eat's ALMAS page is behind a Cloudflare challenge and was **not bypassed**.
- Maydan timed out and is not counted as inspected.
- Lido84's domain is parked for sale and is not usable.
- Alchemist Copenhagen and the first Bavel domain did not resolve; Bavel was correctly reached at `baveldtla.com`.
- This stage did **not** include a full local competitor audit of every Aarau restaurant. The competitive section focuses on cuisine-adjacent international benchmarks and ALMAS's own public footprint.

## 5. Restaurant references — detailed observations

### 01. Noma — https://noma.dk/

**Screenshot:** `noma-desktop.png`, `noma-mobile.png`

**Observed:** A large, quiet photograph of the restaurant's wooden building in a field; the wordmark and a short navigation sit over it. A small card anchors the current chapter ("Copenhagen 2026") and a newsletter prompt. The page is editorial and unhurried; text is used as content, not decoration.

**Why it works:** The restaurant's point of view is communicated through place, season and writing. The interface almost disappears.

**ALMAS takeaway:** Borrow the calm and the confidence, not the text volume. ALMAS needs a much shorter story until the client provides verified history. The "current chapter" card is a useful pattern for opening hours or today's service status.

**Do not borrow:** Long autobiographical essays, seasonal chapter language, or any implication of foraging/fine-dining craft that ALMAS cannot substantiate.

---

### 02. SingleThread — https://singlethreadfarms.com/

**Screenshot:** `singlethread-desktop.png`

**Observed:** A full-bleed, dusk-lit landscape with a tiny centered white logo. Address and booking information sit in a restrained card at the lower right. Serif display type, wide letter-spacing, very few navigation items.

**Why it works:** It sells a place and a feeling before it sells a menu. The scale of the photograph creates immediate premium perception.

**ALMAS takeaway:** The bottom-corner information card is an excellent pattern for address, hours and phone over a hero image. Use it with real ALMAS context — the Bahnhofstrasse, the room, the grill — not a generic landscape.

**Do not borrow:** Rural/farm imagery. ALMAS is an urban restaurant on a main street; pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

### 03. Eleven Madison Park — https://www.elevenmadisonpark.com/

**Screenshot:** `eleven-madison-park-desktop.png`

**Observed:** A cream page, centered wordmark, sparse navigation and a large photograph of people working with produce in a greenhouse. Enormous white space; almost no interface chrome. The menu page URL returned 404, which is a reminder that even flagship sites have broken links.

**Why it works:** Typographic poise and white space signal confidence. Photography is documentary rather than glossy.

**ALMAS takeaway:** Generous margins and a small number of elements per screen. A single documentary image can outperform a collage.

**Do not borrow:** Its extreme minimalism. ALMAS has more practical information to convey — hours, takeaway, menu categories, languages — and needs a warmer, more navigable structure.

### 04. Saffy's — https://www.saffysla.com/

**Screenshot:** `saffys-desktop.png`

**Observed:** Warm cream background, centered logo, compact navigation, and a triptych of generous, colourful plates. Headline: "THIS IS SAFFY'S PLACE — Where you can eat with your hands and drink with your friends." The tone is playful, human and confident.

**Why it works:** It proves that a cuisine-adjacent restaurant can feel premium without black, gold or minimalism. Colour, food and voice do the work. The copy is specific and unpretentious.

**ALMAS takeaway:** This is one of the closest tonal references. Warmth, generosity and a direct invitation are more appropriate than aloof luxury. A single well-written line can do more than a paragraph of brand language.

**Do not borrow:** The very casual, almost diner-like register. ALMAS should be warmer than fine dining but more composed than Saffy's.

## 7. Cross-cutting patterns that matter

### 7.1 Photography is the brand

Across all 17 references, the strongest sites are carried by one or two exceptional images, not by interface decoration. Food, room and place do the persuading.

**Implication:** The prototype must be art-directed around a small, coherent image set — not a large gallery of mismatched stock.

### 7.2 Sparse navigation, visible practical information

Most sites use 4–6 navigation items. The best ones keep address, hours or booking in a slim persistent bar or a hero-corner card.

**Implication:** ALMAS nav: **Menu · Our Kitchen · Visit · Order** (Order only if confirmed), with hours/phone always reachable.

### 7.3 Two dominant palettes

- **Warm light:** cream, bone, sand, muted terracotta (Beddia, Saffy's, EMP, St. JOHN, Zahav).
- **Deep dark:** charcoal, near-black, warm brown, ember (Bavel, Barbary, Karaköy, SingleThread, Schauenstein).

Both work when the photography matches. Mixing them carelessly produces the generic "luxury template" look.

**Implication:** ALMAS's dual-theme foundation is a genuine advantage if each theme has its own image treatment: Porcelain for daylight/menu clarity, Obsidian for evening/atmosphere.

### 7.4 Typography as the premium signal

The references use display serif + neutral sans + small mono/uppercase labels. Ornament is rare. Scale, spacing and cropping create luxury.

**Implication:** The fixed type system is correct. Invest in type scale and spacing discipline rather than graphics.

### 7.5 Editorial menus, not card grids

The strongest menu presentations use category labels, item names, descriptions and prices on a baseline, separated by hairlines or space. Card grids appear mainly on design-gallery sites, not on the best restaurant sites.

**Implication:** Layout 03 should be a true editorial menu, not a grid of dish cards.

### 7.6 Colour can be cultural without being clichéd

Parwana's turquoise, Saffy's cream, River Cafe's blue and Beddia's cream/red show that a confident colour field can be more distinctive than another beige minimal site.

**Implication:** Pomegranate and smoked limestone should be used with conviction in small, controlled areas — not scattered as decoration.

### 7.7 Motion is restrained

The best sites use slow image reveals, gentle hover scale and simple fades. Scroll-jacking, custom cursors and preloaders appear on award sites but not on the strongest hospitality sites.

**Implication:** Motion budget: fades, small translates, image scale on hover, smooth theme transition. Nothing that delays access to the menu.

### 7.8 Honest copy outperforms brand poetry

St. JOHN, Beddia and Saffy's use plain, specific language. The more "luxury" adjectives a site uses, the less premium it feels.

**Implication:** Write like a confident host, not a fragrance ad. No "symphony of flavours", no "journey", no invented heritage.

## 9. Competitive observations

### 9.1 Cuisine-adjacent premium set

Bavel, Zahav, Saffy's, Parwana and The Palomar define the contemporary Middle Eastern/Afghan premium look:

- Warm, earthy or jewel-toned palettes — never black/gold.
- Food photography with generous portions and human hands.
- Storytelling rooted in family, place and hospitality.
- Menus that explain rather than intimidate.
- A balance of cultural specificity and modern restraint.

**ALMAS opportunity:** None of these is a Swiss neighbourhood grill with pizza, pide and kebab. ALMAS can own a distinct position: **the precision of Swiss design applied to the generosity of Afghan/Turkish hospitality** — if the Afghan part is confirmed.

### 9.2 Pizza-as-craft set

Pepe in Grani and Pizzeria Beddia prove that pizza and everyday food can be presented as craft without fine-dining pretension. This is directly relevant to ALMAS's verified menu, which is dominated by kebab, pizza, pide and boxes.

**ALMAS opportunity:** Treat the grill, the dough and the pide as subjects worth photographing and describing precisely. This is more credible than pretending the restaurant is a tasting-menu destination.

### 9.3 Swiss premium set

Schloss Schauenstein, Maison Manesse and Sézanne show Swiss/international premium codes: restraint, full-bleed imagery, precise typography, very little ornament.

**ALMAS opportunity:** Borrow the composure, not the formality. Aarau is not a resort or a hotel; the site should feel like an excellent neighbourhood restaurant with unusually good design.

## 10. What we should deliberately avoid

1. **Black + gold "luxury restaurant" template.** It contradicts the palette and reads as generic.
2. **Gold gradients and ornate flourishes.** They belong to the old logo, not the new system.
3. **Glassmorphism, gradient text, neon glows.** Fashionable, not gastronomic.
4. **Repetitive three-card grids.** They flatten the menu into a template.
5. **Full-screen carousels.** They dilute the hero and hurt performance.
6. **Scroll-jacking, custom cursors, preloaders.** They delay the menu and feel dated.
7. **Stock photos of smiling chefs, fake guests or generic dining rooms.** They destroy trust.
8. **AI-generated food images presented as real dishes.** Misrepresentation.
9. **Invented awards, reviews, history, chef bios or "since 19xx".** Prohibited.
10. **Afghan visual clichés** — carpets, lanterns, ornamental arches, "exotic" script — unless the client explicitly wants a specific cultural reference.
11. **Fake ordering or booking flows.** Only link to real, confirmed providers.
12. **Dotted price leaders and faux-fancy menu language.** The menu should be clear and modern, not a wedding invitation.

## 11. Recommended creative direction — "FACETS"

### 11.1 Concept

A diamond is defined by its cuts. ALMAS is defined by its facets: the charcoal grill, the bread and pide, the welcome, the town, the two languages. The design makes those facets visible through **geometry, light and editing** — not ornament.

The concept gives the brand:

- A ownable geometric language derived from the existing A + diamond logo.
- A reason for the dual light/dark themes.
- A way to be premium without pretending to be fine dining.
- A system that works for photography, menu, visit and future expansion.

### 11.2 Visual system

**Geometry**
- A 45° rotated square (diamond) used as an image mask, a section marker and the counter of the "A".
- Maximum one geometric gesture per screen. No repeating diamond patterns.
- Hairline rules in oxidized silver; generous negative space.

**Colour**
- **Porcelain #EAE5DC** — primary light surface; menu, visit, practical content.
- **Mineral plum #30232F** — primary dark surface and body text on light.
- **Obsidian #191719** — immersive hero and evening sections.
- **Pomegranate #9D454B** — accent only: active nav, price emphasis, small markers. Never large fields.
- **Smoked limestone #D4B99A** — warm secondary surface and image backdrops.
- **Oxidized silver #9CA5A3** — hairlines, metadata, secondary text.

**Typography**
- **Instrument Serif** — headlines and dish names. Tight leading, large sizes, occasional italic for emphasis.
- **DM Sans** — descriptions, navigation, buttons, longer text.
- **IBM Plex Mono** — category numbers, opening hours, sourcing notes, language switcher, price column if tabular.
- No more than three weights per family. No all-caps body text.

**Photography**
- Art direction: ember, charcoal, steam, hands, bread, pomegranate, pistachio, sesame, steel, stone.
- Deep shadows, warm highlights, close crops. Avoid bright white studio food and fake restaurant scenes.
- Hero: one cinematic image with deliberate negative space for type.
- Menu: a small number of ingredient/dish images, not one per item.
- Place: real interior only. Use the client video frames as temporary texture, clearly replaced at Stage 5.
- Prototype stock must be licensed and documented. No AI food imagery.

**Motion**
- 200–400 ms fades and small translates.
- 1.03 image scale on hover.
- Theme transition: 300 ms colour cross-fade.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no loading animation.

**Logo**
- Recreate the A + diamond as a single-colour vector mark.
- Remove the gold gradient, red gemstone rendering and ornamental linework.
- Wordmark: customised Instrument Serif or a drawn serif with a geometric A.
- Lockups: horizontal wordmark, stacked mark, favicon/app icon.
- The diamond may appear as a standalone mark for favicon and social avatar.

### 11.3 Page architecture

**Home**
1. **Hero** — full-bleed image, wordmark, one-line positioning, live open/closed status, scroll cue.
2. **Statement** — two or three verified sentences; no invented history.
3. **From the menu** — three verified dishes presented editorially (e.g. Lamm Grill Teller, Pizza Aarau, Baklava), labelled as menu items, not "signatures" unless the client confirms.
4. **The grill** — an atmospheric image and a short factual paragraph about charcoal/grill preparation only if confirmed; otherwise about the offer.
5. **Sourcing note** — the meat-origin declaration from the menu, presented as a precise mono-typeset note. This is a real, differentiating trust signal.
6. **Visit** — address, hours, phone, map link, takeaway/delivery status.
7. **Footer** — languages, legal, social (if confirmed), credit.

**Menu**
- Editorial intro, then category index and sections.
- Desktop: sticky category index left, menu content right; two columns within long categories.
- Mobile: sticky category chips, single column, prices aligned right.
- Each item: name (Instrument Serif), optional description (DM Sans), price (DM Sans tabular or Plex Mono).
- Family pizza 40 cm shown as a secondary price, not a separate item.
- Allergen note only when verified.

**Our Kitchen** (working title)
- A short, factual page about the offer and the people, built only from verified information.
- If the client provides a story, this becomes the emotional core. Until then, it stays concise and honest.

**Visit**
- Address, map (click-to-load), hours, phone, transport/parking only if verified.
- Takeaway/delivery explanation with real links only.
- No reservation form unless the client confirms a reservation system.

### 11.4 Why this direction wins

- It is **specific to ALMAS** — the diamond is already in the brand, not an imported concept.
- It satisfies both required layouts without looking like two different sites.
- It makes the dual theme meaningful rather than decorative.
- It is **premium without deception**, which protects the client's reputation and the agency's credibility.
- It is buildable with the available assets and can scale when real photography and Afghan dishes arrive.

## 13. Content implications

The design cannot be finalised until these content questions are answered:

1. **Afghan dishes** — do they exist? Which dishes, names and prices? This determines whether the homepage can say "Afghan kitchen".

3. **Hours** — full weekly schedule, holidays, lunch/dinner split.
4. **Service model** — dine-in, takeaway, delivery, drive-through, reservations. Which are real and which should be promoted?

6. **German** — approve German + English as primary, with Swedish optional.
7. **Photography** — approve licensed stock for the prototype and schedule a real shoot before launch.
8. **Domain** — who owns `almasrestaurant.ch`, and can the broken site be replaced?
9. **Reviews** — may the Google rating be referenced, with a link and date?
10. **Logo** — approve the refined single-colour A + diamond direction.
